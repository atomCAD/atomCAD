Seeing Electrons in atomCAD

The orbitals node

A planned feature that shows the electronic structure of a design directly on the atoms: intrinsic bond orbitals, canonical frontier orbitals, spin density and potential maps, bond orders and energies, and a check of whether the electrons agree with the bonds we drew. This page is about what you would get and how it would feel to use. The engineering detail lives elsewhere.

What you would see

You build or carve a structure in atomCAD as today, for example a small silicon cluster around a defect. You mark the atoms you care about with a tag, by clicking them in the atom editor. After a calculation, the same structure carries its orbitals, and you choose which ones to draw from a list that is organised by atoms and bonds. All of this lives in one new node, orbitals: wire the structure into it, and the calculation settings, the orbital list and the display toggles are its panel.

What one calculation gives

  • Bond orbitals (intrinsic bond orbitals, IBOs). The occupied orbitals localized by the IAO/IBO scheme: one per bond, lone pair or unpaired electron, each on one or two atoms. Selecting an atom or a tag lists only the orbitals on it.
  • Frontier orbitals (canonical). HOMO, LUMO and the singly occupied orbital as the SCF produces them, with their energies, for the questions that need energies.
  • Spin density. Where the unpaired electron lives and how far it reaches into the lattice.
  • Electrostatic potential map. The molecule's surface coloured by potential, red where it is negative and blue where it is positive. One toggle.
  • Bond orders. A number on every bond in the list (0.98, 1.87) and a free-valence number on every atom, how many bonds it is short of. Both come free with the orbitals.
  • Energy. The total energy leaves the node as a number the network can subtract: adsorbed minus free, after minus before.

The check: do the electrons agree with the drawing?

atomCAD is unusual in that its bonds are drawn by the designer. A localized-orbital calculation produces its own bonding picture. The feature overlays the two on the structure, so a disagreement is visible at a glance instead of buried in a table.

drawn bond, confirmed by an orbital drawn bond with no orbital behind it orbital bond that was not drawn unpaired electron or lone pair on an atom

For a process step this is the question that matters: after the tool operation, are the bonds we intended the bonds that formed, and did a radical end up where we expect it?

The check has two halves

The bond comparison judges the electrons at the positions we drew. It catches wrong bonds, missing bonds, wrong bond orders and misplaced unpaired electrons. It cannot catch a structure that is consistent as drawn but not what the atoms would do: an unreconstructed surface with no dimer bonds in the drawing passes.

The forces, which the same calculation gives almost for free, answer that second question. atomCAD names the largest force and draws an arrow on every atom. Large arrows on a surface say "these atoms want to move", the signature of a reconstruction that was not applied or a strained cover. Where they would end up needs a relaxation, which stays with the simulation tools.

How it would be used

Step 1 · in atomCAD
Tag and export

Tag the atoms of interest, pick a method and basis from a short list, press Export job in the orbitals node. atomCAD writes a job folder.

Step 2 · one command
Run the calculation

One PySCF command on the job folder. A 70-atom cluster takes on the order of ten minutes on a laptop. Nothing to install in atomCAD.

Step 3 · in atomCAD
Load and look

Press Load results. The orbitals appear on the structure. If the structure changed since the job, atomCAD says so. Want more orbitals drawn? Tick them, Export job, run, Load again: seconds, not minutes.

Later, the simulation team's compute could replace step 2 without changing steps 1 and 3; the job folder is the interface.

What comes first, and what later

  • First: the calculation loop, the orbital list by tag with bond orders, the lobes, the frontier energies, spin density and the potential map, the energy as a number, the forces.
  • Then: the drawn-versus-computed bond overlay, atomic charges and spin populations on the atoms.
  • Then: clicking atoms and bonds in the 3D view to filter the list, and following one orbital across the steps of a process.
Where your opinion decides the design
  • Which of the three pictures (bond orbitals, frontier orbitals, spin density) do you reach for first, and on which structures: the finished defect, the chemisorbed precursor, or the tool at the surface?
  • Is the drawn-versus-computed bond check useful, or do you already know the bonding and want only shapes and energies?
  • Which method and basis should the defaults be, and is a GGA functional acceptable for pictures or do you want a hybrid quoted?
  • For an unpaired electron, do you want separate alpha and beta orbitals, or one picture per bond with the unpaired one singled out?
  • What is missing from the mockup that you would need before trusting a picture from it?
